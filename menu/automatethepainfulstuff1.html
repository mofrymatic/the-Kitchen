§ 0 — Free Body Diagram

A workspace holds a human and its computing systems in equilibrium across three flows: thermal, chemical, and mechanical. Nociception is the signal that an equilibrium is being violated.

Boring work is the apprenticeship of physical skill and should stay human. Painful work — that which damages the human form faster than it can heal — is what we delegate to machines. This volume enumerates the classes of painful work an engineer can build against, in priority order: the envelope first (immune system), then hostile spaces (zero adaptation), then cumulative load (Trecovery > Tincident).

Each chapter is scoped for engineers who will actually build the mechanism. Structural: what does it grip, and against what reaction? Mechanical: what is the duty cycle and what fails first? Electrical: what is the power budget in a place with no outlet? Robotic: what is the minimum viable replaceability — reliably enough to replace, not merely assist?

§ ∞.1 · Redesign First

Minimum viable replaceability gates every automation. If cost of ownership exceeds the human's adaptation cost, or reliability falls short of full replacement, the answer is to redesign the task — jigs, geometry, task rotation — not to build a robot.

§ ∞.2 · Ambient, not Invoked

ATBS chapters are discrete scripts. Painful physical work is often ambient — the 03:00 spore, the daily 2° stratification. The primary deliverable of Part I is a workspace daemon, not a harvest bot.

§ ∞.3 · Push the Edge

The tractable ML problem clusters proposals in Part IV. The high-value work is manipulation-hard, Parts I–III. Build there; the field is thinner and the deficit you close is your own.
