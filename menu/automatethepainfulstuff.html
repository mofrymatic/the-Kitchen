Introduction

“You just replaced two months of Julys in that crawlspace.” A friend had spent three summers dragging a shop-vac through a 92°F attic chasing a mildew smell to its source. The fourth summer, a tethered rover mapped every truss bay with a $40 gas sensor and a thermal camera, found the leak in an afternoon, and never asked to be paid in Gatorade.

This is the counterpart to Automate the Boring Stuff with Python. Boring work is the apprenticeship of physical skill and should stay human; painful work — that which damages the human form faster than it can heal — is what we delegate to machines.

The Industrial Paradigm Shift

Every hobbyist pain point in this manual has an industrial twin — the same free body diagram, the same three equilibria, run at a scale where the deficit compounds against a balance sheet instead of a single spine. A rover clearing mildew from a residential attic and a rover clearing thermal dead zones on a data center floor are running the same control problem. The scale changes the stakes, not the physics.

Each chapter below is paired: a domestic entry point for the n-of-1 trial, and its industrial equivalent at fleet scale. The line beneath each pair names the shared infrastructure control — the thing you are actually engineering, independent of which client is paying for it.

Domestic / Hobbyist

Low capital, high learning rate. The reference design proven here de-risks everything to its right.

Industrial / Scaled

Same failure mode, fleet scale, high capital. Failures found here tell you what to defend against at home.

A Note on Scope Chapters are ordered by priority, not difficulty. Part I — the envelope of the workspace itself — comes first because it defends the substrate on which every other kind of work is done. The robot is the immune system of the workspace, not a servant performing chores.
